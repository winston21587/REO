STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURES MANUAL
INTRODUCTION
In 2013, the Research Ethics Oversight Committee (REOC) was established with the
primary goal of providing exemplary research ethics reviews and practices within the
academic institution. The term "oversight" was included in its acronym, as the committee
was tasked with overseeing all research activities across all colleges in the institution. The
SOP Manual of the REOC outlines essential components, including its inception, institutional
policy on ethical review of research involving human participants, governance structure,
mandate, decision-making processes, and administrative support. The REOC conducts
ethical reviews for undergraduate and graduate programs, as well as externally funded
research. The success of the REOC can be attributed to the dedication and hard work of the
pioneering leaders and supporters of research within the institution.
To ensure that ethical standards are upheld in research involving human participants, REOC
operates within the guidelines set forth by the university's institutional policy on ethical
review. This policy mandates that no research projects or proposals can be implemented
without first being granted ethical clearance. Additionally, all students pursuing diploma and
higher education degrees must submit a thesis or dissertation output that has undergone
ethical review by REOC.
The presence of REOC within the university is crucial in ensuring that research activities are
conducted in a manner that protects the rights, safety, and welfare of all stakeholders. This
commitment to ethical research practices is in accordance with R.A. 10532 of 2013, which
established the Philippine National Health Research Systems (PNHRS) and created the
Philippine Health Ethics Review Board as the national policy-making body in research ethics.
The leadership of Dr. Milabel E. Ho and Dr. Jocelyn P. Pedroso, both faculty members of the
College of Agriculture, was instrumental in facilitating and refining ethics review at the
institution. In fact, the first SOP Manual for REOC (Version 1, 2015) was drafted and
approved by the Board of Regents during their tenure.
In terms of the REOC's organizational structure, it is important to note that the committee's
authority is derived from the higher governing authorities, including the Board of Regents,
University President, and Vice- President of RESEL. The selection and appointment of the
REOC Chair, Vice Chair, Secretariat, and other members are subject to the approval of
these higher authorities. The organizational structure of the committee outlines the roles and
responsibilities of its members, with the panel of reviewers being a key component in the
review of undergraduate, graduate, and institutionally and externally funded research.
Overall, the governance structure of the REOC is designed to ensure accountability and
effective execution of the committee's mandate.

The Research Ethics Oversight Committee (REOC) at Western Mindanao State University is
responsible for providing ethical review services to stakeholders. The committee follows a
set of 27 standard operating procedures (SOPs), which are outlined in the manual and
consist of a recommended template with ten sections. These SOPs provide a framework for
the committee's decision-making processes and help ensure that ethical standards are met
in all research involving human respondents. The committee members and panel of
reviewers are given full authority in the conduct of review and the granting of ethical
clearance to worthy stakeholders, ensuring the authenticity and integrity of their activities.
The independence of decision-making in the REOC is a crucial aspect of its functioning. The
committee's offices and panel of reviewers are granted full authority in the conduct of
reviews and granting ethical clearance to stakeholders. The academic institution places its
trust in the authenticity and integrity of the members' decisions, whether they approve,
disapprove, or continue with a project.
This autonomy allows the REOC to uphold ethical standards without outside influence,
ensuring that stakeholders' interests are protected. It also reinforces the committee's
accountability, as they are responsible for ensuring that all research projects meet ethical
guidelines.
The REOC's independence is reflected in the organizational structure, which clearly outlines
the scope of the committee's duties and responsibilities. The structure also emphasizes the
committee's accountability and the importance of ethical decision-making. Ultimately, the
independence of the committee supports the integrity and quality of research conducted by
stakeholders.
The research conducted by the REOC is greatly facilitated by the administrative support
provided by Western Mindanao State University. As a higher education institution, the
university is acutely aware of the challenges faced by its constituents, including poverty and
threats to peace and security, and is committed to addressing these issues through its
research agenda. The university administration fully supports high-end research conducted
in the region by providing a range of resources, including technological knowledge,
manpower, academic expertise, and other forms of support. These efforts are instrumental in
expediting the research process and helping to alleviate the ongoing pressures faced by the
public, ultimately contributing to a better future for the region. By providing this support, the
university demonstrates its commitment to fostering innovative research that can have a
significant impact on the social and economic development of the region.

template consisting of a header and 10 sections be utilized. The purpose of this template is
to provide guidance to the REOC throughout the process of creating each SOP, with each
section containing specific questions that should be answered by the committee. In some

To provide a solid foundation for the activity and procedures outlined in each SOP, Section
1, titled "Policy Statement," should include the institutional or committee policies upon which
they are based. In addition, this section may also reference specific provisions from relevant
international and national guidelines that are pertinent to the activity at hand. By including
this information, the REOC can ensure that their procedures align with established policies
and guidelines, promoting ethical and effective research practices.
To ensure clarity and consistency in the development of each SOP, a recommended
template is utilized, consisting of a header and 10 sections. Section 2, titled "Objective,"
serves to clearly state the purpose of the activity for which the SOP is being developed. For
example, in the case of an SOP titled "Preparing for a Meeting," the objective may be stated
as "Preparing for a meeting aims to ensure that all meeting documents and necessary
logistics are available during the meeting." By clearly stating the objective, the REOC can
ensure that the SOP is tailored to achieve the specific goal of the activity in question.
Section 3 outlines the boundaries of the activity and is based on the workflow described in
Section 5. It identifies the initial and final steps involved in the activity. The scope should be
defined clearly to ensure that the activity remains focused and achieves its objectives. The
scope may include any limitations or exclusions that are relevant to the activity. It is
important to note that the scope may vary depending on the context and nature of the
activity.
Section 4 of the template is dedicated to outlining the workflow involved in the activity. This
may take the form of a diagram or matrix that briefly illustrates the different steps involved in
the process, along with the individuals responsible for each step. The diagram may be in the
form of a flowchart, utilizing standard symbols such as circles (to indicate start and end
points), rectangles (to denote specific steps), and diamonds (for decision points). The person
or persons responsible for each action is identified, typically using verb nouns such as
"receipt of," "submission of," "conduct of," "distribution of," "filing of," and "approval of."

Statement of Policy
The Research Ethics Oversight Committee (REOC) shall have a Chair, Vice-Chair,
REOC Secretary, Ethics Review Panel (ERP) Chairs and members, and two (2)
REOC staff. The Chair, Vice Chair, REOC Secretary, ERP members except for the
non-scientist and non-affiliated must be regular faculty of the University who shall be
recommended by the Vice President for RESEL and designated by the University
President. They must have attended at least the Basic Training for Ethics Protocol
Review. The non-scientist members may be Visiting Lecturers or Office Clerks of the
University with a bachelor’s degree and have attended Basic Ethics Training. The
non-affiliated members may come from other institutions and have attended at least
Basic Ethics Training. The two (2) REOC staff may be hired as Contract of Service
(COS) or Job Order (JO).

2.

Objective of the activity
The Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) on the Selection and Appointment of
REOC Members aims to ensure that the composition of the REOC complies with
international, national and institutional guidelines that emphasize and account for the
relevant expertise and appropriate representation of members across disciplines and
sectors. Moreover, the same SOP identifies the constitution of the REOC to include
the non-scientists and non-affiliated members.

3.

Scope
This Standard Operating Procedure applies only to the designation of REOC
members (i.e., REOC Chair, Vice Chair, REOC Secretary, ERP chairs and members,
non-scientist and non-affiliated members). This SOP starts with the recommendation
of the REOC members and ends with filing of the pertinent documents.

4.

Description of Procedures
As provided in the workflow presented above, the following steps are described.
5.1 Selection and appointment of the WMSU REOC Chair
Step 1 – Recommendation. The REOC Chair will be recommended by the
Vice President for RESEL to the University President.
Step 2 – Receipt of Special Order. Upon the approval of the President, a
special order will be issued to the designated REOC chair. The special order

Step 4 – Confidentiality agreement and conflict of interest disclosure.

Step 5 – Office Assumption. The designated REOC Chair, upon receipt of

of the new REOC members will be concurred by the Vice President for
Research, Extension Services and External Linkages.

recommendation provided by the REOC Chair with the concurrence of the
VP-RESEL, the President of the University issues a special order designating
the identified person to serve as REOC members (i.e., Vice Chair, REOC
Secretary, ERP chairs and members, including non-scientist and nonaffiliated). The special order enumerates the designee’s roles and
responsibilities, including release time and other benefits, if applicable.

Within 2 days, the REOC staff shall forward the Appointment (Special Order)
to the newly designated REOC member.
Step 5 – Signing of pertinent documents. Upon assuming office, the REOC
member is required to sign a conflict of interest, disclosure, and confidentiality
agreement.
Step 6 – Office Assumption. The designated REOC member, upon receipt
of the Special Order, assumes office and shall perform the duties and
responsibilities stipulated in the Special Order.
Step 7 – Filing of pertinent documents. The REOC staff files appointment
document and comprehensive curriculum vitae.

Statement of Policy
The REOC shall invite an independent consultant whose expertise is not represented
in the current membership but is needed in a study under review. He/she need not be
affiliated with the institution.

2.

Objective of the Activity
This activity aims to ensure that the appointment of independent consultants
conforms with institutional practice and complements the pool of expertise in the
REOC.

3.

Scope
This SOP specifically pertains to the selection and designation of independent
consultants in the review of research protocols of the REOC. This SOP begins with
the identification of the study that requires an independent consultant and ends with
the inclusion of the name of the Independent Consultant in the pool of consultants.

Description of Procedures
Step 1 - Identification of the study that requires an independent
consultant: Either the REOC Chair or REOC Vice Chair identifies the study
that requires an expertise necessary in the review of a research protocol and
that may not be provided by the current members of the REOC.
Step 2 - Identification of the independent consultant: The REOC Chair or
REOC Vice Chair refers to the roster of specialists in the institution or in other
institutions for the necessary expertise and selects the appropriate expert.
S/he instructs the REOC staff to prepare the letter of invitation.
Step 3 - Invitation of the independent consultant: The REOC Staff
prepares a LET-009 – letter of invitation containing the Terms of Reference
for signature of the Chair and sends this to the identified expert. The letter of
invitation contains a section for acceptance of the invitation (conforme) and
FR-003 – Form for conflict of disclosure and confidentiality agreement.
Step 4 - Receipt of the signed invitation, confidentiality agreement and
conflict interest disclosure: The staff receives the signed invitation,
Confidentiality and Conflict of Interest Disclosure agreement and files this in
the appropriate folder.
Step 5 - Appointment of independent consultant: Upon receipt of the
acceptance of the invitation, the REOC Staff prepares a memorandum
addressed to the WMSU President requesting for appointment of independent
consultant (LET-009). The request for appointment shall be recommended by
the VP-RESEL for approval by the WMSU President and release of
appointment.
Step 6 - Receipt of the appointment of independent consultant: Upon
receipt of appointment from the Office of the WMSU President, a copy of the
appointment shall be provided to the independent consultant.
Step 7 - Inclusion in the pool of independent consultant: The REOC Staff
enters the name of the new independent consultants in the appropriate
database containing name, expertise, institution and date of appointment.

Statement of Policy
The WMSU REOC shall require submission of a set of relevant documents for an
application for ethical review. Applications for ethical review may be submitted
through several modes (e.g. hand-carried by the researcher, through email or
through the use of an online-platform provided). Only complete submission of
documents is accepted for ethical review. A preliminary evaluation shall determine
whether a research protocol is exempted from or needs to undergo ethical review
based on the NEGRIHP 2022.

2.

Objective of the activity
The Management of initial submissions ensures that relevant documents submitted
are complete, properly documented, and properly evaluated to substantiate
appropriate action or type of review.

3.

Scope
The REOC staff shall accept the research protocol submitted by the faculty and
students for initial review. This SOP begins with the receipt of study documents for
initial review and ends with entry of protocol information in the database.

Description of Procedures
Step 1 – Receipt of research protocols for initial review and
determination of the completeness of submission: The REOC staff
receives the relevant application documents for initial review of protocol and
determination of completeness of documents. Acceptance of application for
ethics review will be from 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM with noon break. An
Application Form FR-002 attached to every application document shall be
used by the REOC staff to determine completeness of the submitted
documents. In the event that the documents submitted are incomplete, the
REOC staff shall return the protocol package.
Step 2 – Entry into logbook: Details of all received an application for ethics
review documents shall be logged into the WMSU REOC logbook by the
REOC staff. Details shall include the (1) specific date and time of receipt, (2)
title of the study, (3) name of the proponent, (4) date of submission, (5) name
of the receiver and (6) action. Likewise, the name and signature of the
individual who actually submitted the documents in case s/he is not the
proponent shall also be included.
Step 3 – Coding of documents: Each study with complete documents for
review received by the REOC staff shall be assigned with a code. The code
includes information on the year of submission and a series number. The
series number will refer to the order of submission when that specific protocol
was received ( e.g. If Ms. Juana DeLa Cruz submitted a protocol in 2023 and
it was the 2nd protocol submitted for the year, then it will bear the code 202302). Further, the code shall also serve as the ID number of the protocol and
cannot be assigned to any other protocol. When referring to the protocol in
communications or presentations, the code shall be used.
Step 4 - Determination of type of Review/Action: The WMSU- REOC
Chair/Vice Chair conducts a preliminary review of the protocol to determine
whether it will undergo Expedited, Full or be Exempted from Review. If the
protocol qualifies for the exemption, the REOC Chair/Vice Chair directs the
REOC staff to follow SOP on Exemption. However, if the REOC Chair/Vice
Chair determines that the protocol should undergo either Full or Expedited
review, then the REOC staff proceeds to follow either SOP 04 on Expedited
Review or SOP 05 Full Review.
Step 5 - Preparation of a Protocol Folder: The REOC staff files the protocol
documents in a protocol folder and labels it accordingly. (SOP 23 Managing
Active Files)

Statement of Policy
Exempt from Review is the term used to denote that a protocol does not need to
undergo full or expedited review after an initial review conducted by the REOC
Chair/Vice Chair. The following are the characteristics of the protocols which shall be
exempted from the review (adapted from National Ethical Guidelines for Research
Involving Human Participants (NEGRIHP 2022)
a.

Protocols with no human participants or do not involve more than minimal
risks or harms;

b.

Protocols for institutional quality assurance purposes, evaluation of public
service programs, public health surveillance, educational evaluation activities
and consumer acceptability tests;

c.

Research that only includes interactions involving survey procedures,
interview procedures, or observation of public behaviour (including visual or
auditory recording), if the following criteria are met:
c. 1. There will be no disclosure of the human participants’ responses outside
the research that could reasonably place the participants at risk of criminal or
civil liability or be damaging to their financial standing, employability or
reputation and;
c. 2. The investigator records the information obtained in such a manner that
the identity of the human participants cannot readily be ascertained directly or
through identifiers linked to the participants.

d.

2.

Protocols that involve the use of publicly available data or information.

Objective of the activity
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) serves as a guide for a consistent
process of exempting protocols from review, specifically those involving no human
participants or non-vulnerable populations with minimal or low risk for harm.

FULL REVIEW

SOP No.

6

24-Mar-2025

Statement of Policy
A full review shall be conducted for research protocols with more than minimal risk to
the participants, particularly if they are members of a vulnerable group, or when a
study generates vulnerability to the participants. Full review shall be conducted
through a primary reviewer system. If necessary, independent consultants and the
proponents shall be invited during the meeting to clarify certain issues before a
documented communication is finalized. The decision shall be communicated to the
proponent within four (4) weeks after submission of the required documents to
include post approval reports (eg. amendments).

2.

Objective of the activity
A full review seeks to ensure compliance with technical and ethical standards in the
conduct of research involving human participants and identifiable human data and
materials.

3.

Scope
This SOP applies to initial, re-submissions and post-approval submissions which are
classified as entailing more than minimal risk to study participants or whose
participants belong to vulnerable groups. This SOP begins with the assignment of
ERP or independent consultant/s (if needed) and ends with the filing of protocolrelated documents.

Statement of Policy
The REOC shall require the submission of proposed amendments for review and
approval before their implementation. This requirement shall be explicitly stated in
the LET-005 – Approval Letter.

2.

Objective of the activity
The Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) on the Review of Amendment aims to
ensure that the conduct of the study is in compliance with the approved protocol such
that any change such as amendments does not impact safety and welfare of study
participants.

3.

Scope

by the proponent while the study is on-going. This SOP begins with the receipt and
entry of the submission of amendment in the logbook of incoming documents then

There shall be two types of REOC meetings, namely: the regular and the special

decisions regarding the study protocols and REC operations and to be informed of

documents the date received, source (person who sent communication),
subject, person who received communication, and action taken (with details

stores and files all copies of the incoming/outgoing communications in the
appropriate filing system for protocol-related communications to the study
protocol file while non-protocol-related documents are filed in the appropriate
administrative file. The staff logs all related information for protocol related
documents in the database for protocols. The REOC Secretariat oversees the
storing and filing of all incoming/outgoing communications by checking its
contents regularly.

Statement of Policy
Access to REOC confidential files shall be regulated and limited to REOC Members
and Staff. Other persons such as institutional authorities, regulatory agencies, or
sponsors with legitimate interests on the files can be allowed access only with proper
justification. Researchers shall be allowed access only to their own protocol files if
requested.
Confidential files shall include study protocol-related documents (e.g. protocols, case
report forms, informed consent documents, scientific documents, expert opinions or
reviews), meeting minutes, decisions, action letters/notification of committee
decision, approval letters, and study protocol-related communications. Confidentiality
of documents is determined by the policies under the data privacy act of 2012.
Moreover, access to REOC confidential documents is subject to the following
limitations:
• REOC Members and staff who have signed a Confidentiality Agreement and
a Conflict of Interest Disclosure can request confidential documents outside
of regular protocol review access.
• Non-members can request access to specific documents by submitting a
formal written request. The REOC staff will provide a copy of the Agreement
Form for Non-members requesting copies of REOC Documents, which must
be completed and signed by the person making the request.
• Regulatory authorities have full access to REOC documents if it is within their
mandates and upon reasonable notice to make the files available signed by
the regulatory authority’s recognized official.

2.

Objective of the activity
Documents submitted to REOC are treated with confidentiality. The management of
requests for access to confidential files helps to protect the rights of the researchers
and research participants while also enhancing the REOCs’ credibility and integrity.

which begins with the request for accessing confidential files with appropriate

Statement of Policy
Queries and complaints from clients, patients or research participants shall be
attended to promptly and appropriately while exercising due diligence. The nature of
queries shall determine whether they can be answered by REOC staff or referred to

Managing queries and complaints aims to promote public trust and confidence in the
institution, especially in REOC and to ensure that the rights and well-being of the
participants are attended to.

families in studies that have received ethical approval from the REOC. And this
begins with the receipt, logging, and acknowledgement of queries and complaints
and ends with the logging of the response and inclusion in the agenda of the REOC
meeting.

Conflict of Interest
